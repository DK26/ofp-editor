Lens: encounter and pacing. Use it when you pick or write a complication, phase intensity, enemy behaviour, warning cue or mood. Choose among what the step offers.

- Does tension build, peak, then truly release? A release is a breath, not another crisis.
- Before each deadly moment, is there a sign the player can read: engines heard, a flare, a radio report?
- Can the squad answer this threat with its own gear? If not, avoid it rather than fight.
- When the player acts, does the enemy answer: search, call for help, fall back, flank?
- If spotted, can the player tell why and hear the response come in waves?
- Does the world keep its own clock, with each deadline announced more than once?
- After a death, would the player blame their own choice?
- Do allies help without winning the fight for the player?
- Does every long walk carry something: a sound, a report, a small decision?
- Is the hardest moment near the end, with no music while the shooting lasts?
- Is it built only from the step's units and places?

Cue before armour, good: "Diesel engines idling somewhere past the treeline. Then nothing."
Flat: "Suddenly, three tanks appear out of nowhere and open fire!"
Borrow the difference, not the words.
