Lens: dialogue and radio. Use it when you write a radio call, banter or a scene line.

- Radio: the station called, then "this is" and the caller; one fact per line; end with over (reply needed) or out (none), never both.
- Is each line on the right channel? Radio reaches someone far away; shouts to the squad are voice.
- Does every line fit the moment the step describes: who ambushes whom, what just happened?
- If someone asks a question, does someone answer it?
- Does it mention only units, places and events the step gives? No new vehicles, no promised support, no unnamed destinations.
- Does the speaker sound like their voice card: one trait, one want, one way of speaking, the habit used sparingly?
- Could you tell who is speaking without the name?
- Under fire, do lines get shorter? Is a loss one line, not a speech?
- Is the enemy heard as soldiers, with no nationality or accent the step does not give?
- Are the words right for the story's year?

Good: "{hq}, this is {me}. Contact, {count} trucks at {place}, infantry getting out. Over."
Flat: "Command, we've got a situation here! Enemy forces everywhere, total chaos, we need backup now!"
Borrow the difference, not the words.
