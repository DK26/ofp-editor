Lens: mission concept. Use it when you pick or write a mission's task, site, objective or conditions. Choose among what the step offers.

- What is this mission for? Name one experience from the step's list; all else serves it.
- Why does it matter to the wider operation?
- Is the objective a checkable end state: a thing destroyed, a place held, a person reached? Not "kill them all" or "destroy what you can".
- Is there a survivable failure with its own ending?
- Does the player choose the way in, and is the way out a named place from the step?
- Does the site offer a named feature to steer by?
- Can the squad answer each listed threat with its own gear? If not, avoid it.
- Does the enemy have a reason to be there and a way to react?
- Is there a moment the player decides, not just executes?
- Is the memorable moment made by a choice or a character, never a scripted death?
- Defending? Prefer time to prepare.

Why it matters, good: "If the bridge at {place} stands, their armour crosses by dawn and catches our column on the road."
Flat: "This mission is vital to the success of the entire operation."
Borrow the difference, not the words.
