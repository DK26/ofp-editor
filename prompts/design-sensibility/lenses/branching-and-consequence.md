Lens: branching and consequence. Use it when you rank choices or outcomes, write effects, or write lines that react to earlier results.

- Does each option cost something visible (time, men, gear, trust) and have a sound reason to pick it?
- Does each named decision send the story somewhere different, or change what a later mission uses?
- Are one mission's outcomes different endings, not parts of the same ending?
- If the player fails, or loses a vehicle or the leader, does the story go on somewhere worth playing?
- Is each effect on declared variables only, in their declared form? Wounded is not dead.
- Is every later line true on every path that reaches it? Never confirm what may have failed.
- Does the next briefing show what changed, and why? No hidden score decides the story.
- Are the fallen named, and missed later?
- Do allies' help and anger follow the player's choices, without allies winning for the player?
- Is a choice of conscience about refusing or protecting, never about committing an atrocity?

Good: "The fuel spent at {place} means no armour tonight. {name}'s section goes in alone."
Flat: "Your earlier choices have had an impact. Choose wisely, soldier, for every decision matters."
Borrow the difference, not the words.
